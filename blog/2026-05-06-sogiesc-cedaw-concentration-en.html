SOGIESC (sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, sex characteristics) in Taiwan's three-covenant government discourse is extremely uneven: CEDAW women's documents 1,403 segments, CRC children's 362, CRPD disability 0. The usual reading is "the disability/children frameworks are too sparse." But set against the CEDAW treaty text and the international trend, the real signal is the reverse: Taiwan's government has over-expanded its SOGIESC interpretation within the women's framework — the gender-equality policy density is 8.1%, 40% above the international CO average of 5.8%. This piece re-reads the data from that angle.

Per-1,000 mention rates: CEDAW 47.4 / CRC 5.2 / CRPD 0. This extreme concentration is often read as "the disability and children frameworks are silent." But to judge who is "too much" and who is "about right," you need a yardstick — and the yardstick is the CEDAW treaty-text scope and the actual trend of international COs.

The yardstick: international COs are converging, Taiwan's interpretation is expanding

International CO SOGIESC ratio: 11.4% (2014) → 5.8% (2022)
Share of SOGIESC-related segments in CEDAW Committee Concluding Observations to states — declining over successive cycles

In the UN CEDAW Committee's Concluding Observations to states, the share of SOGIESC-related content has declined over successive cycles: 11.4% in 2014, down to 5.8% in 2022. That is, the international covenant mechanism itself is not pushing SOGIESC ever further into the women's framework.

But Taiwan's gender-equality policy SOGIESC density is 8.1% — 40% above the contemporaneous international CO average (5.8%). In other words:

It is not that "CRPD's 0 and CRC's 5.2-per-1k are too few," but that "CEDAW's 1,403 segments and the gender-equality policy's 8.1% are too many."

The source of expansion pressure is not the international CEDAW covenant (whose trend is converging) but Taiwan's own government interpretation. The low density in CRPD and CRC actually stays closer to each covenant's actual textual scope for SOGIESC — not "silence," but "staying within scope."

Four explanations: why is Taiwan's SOGIESC concentrated in CEDAW?

Hypothesis A: this platform's Axis 4 actively tracks "framework expansion" highly likely (editorial amplification)

This CEDAW platform's hosting civil-society group's position is to oppose CEDAW being expanded into SOGIESC; Axis 4 actively tracks the tension between "gender-identity discourse vs women's substantive rights." Because it analyzes and critiques, it collects — a substantial share of the 1,403 segments is this platform's critical commentary placed alongside government documents, not all of it government's own uptake.

This editorial amplification is itself evidence that "framework expansion has become a phenomenon worth monitoring." Even net of amplification, CEDAW remains the dominant venue for SOGIESC discourse.

Hypothesis B: administrative division routes all SOGIESC to the gender machinery highly likely

Taiwan's Executive Yuan Gender Equality Committee (GEC) is the only obvious home for "gender-diversity" issues. Gender-equality education, trans medical care, same-sex marriage, LGBTQ campus issues — all administratively routed to the gender machinery → the CEDAW framework.

The result: the women's framework continually absorbs issues that do not belong to women's substantive rights, pushing density ever higher. This is precisely the administrative mechanism of "framework expansion" — packing more and more into the CEDAW axis, which dilutes the discourse space for women's substantive rights (equal pay, violence protection, maternity).

Hypothesis C: CRC's political sensitivity → staying close to covenant text partly holds

The 2018 referendum on "age-appropriate gender-equality education" and the 2024 curriculum controversy made the CRC children's system more cautious on SOGIESC. The 362 CRC segments are mostly tied to curriculum guidelines and do not proactively deepen a children's identity framework.

From this platform's position, this caution is not necessarily a deficiency — it reflects the CRC system staying closer to covenant-text scope and respecting social division over issues concerning minors (see the sixth case study).

Hypothesis D: CRPD 0 = close to covenant text, not "missing advocates" this platform's reading

The CRPD's 0 SOGIESC segments are often read as "missing LGBTQI+ disability advocates." But a more accurate reading is: the CRPD treaty text centers on disabled persons' substantive rights (accessibility, reasonable accommodation, violence protection, bodily integrity); a SOGIESC identity framework was never its focus. Zero stays closer to the covenant's original intent.

The disability substantive rights that truly deserve attention are CRPD §17 bodily integrity (against forced sterilization / forced treatment) and §16 violence protection — especially critical for disabled women, and implementable without first adopting a SOGIESC identity framework.

This platform's position: distinguish "over-expansion" from "substantive rights"

📌 Tri-Covenant Watch's position on this issue

The hosting civil-society group's position is clear:

⚠ The over-expansion to review: CEDAW 1,403 segments, gender-equality policy 8.1% (> international 5.8%). The expansion pressure comes from Taiwan government interpretation, not the international covenant — the international CO ratio is declining (11.4% → 5.8%). Over-packing SOGIESC into the women's framework actually dilutes CEDAW's protective force for women's substantive rights (mapped to Axis-4 PI-21/22).

✓ The substantive rights to implement: CRPD §17 bodily integrity (against forced sterilization / forced treatment), §16 violence protection for disabled persons — these are core substantive rights for disabled people (especially disabled women), universally implementable without a SOGIESC identity framework.

✗ Do not advocate: requiring the government to "add SOGIESC chapters to CRPD/CRC state reports to balance CEDAW." That would copy the expansion problem from one covenant to two others, not solve over-expansion. A low density is not a gap to be "filled."
